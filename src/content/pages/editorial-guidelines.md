<p>Tech Media Arch is an independent blog run by Jacob S. Olsen from Denmark. This page explains how the articles are made, what rules they follow, and what to do if you find an error. It describes what actually happens, not what we would like to happen.</p>
<h2>Two kinds of articles</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Older articles</strong> (before late September 2026): Jacob chose each subject and the search phrases it answers. Many start from a video by an independent creator.</li>
<li><strong>AI-written articles</strong> (from late September 2026): written by a language model (Google Gemini) following the rules below. They are published automatically, and Jacob does not read every article before it goes live. Each of these articles says so in the box about how it was made, at the bottom of the article.</li>
</ul>
<h2>How an AI-written article is made</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>A real question.</strong> It starts from questions people type into Google. Questions an existing article already answers are skipped, so the site does not publish the same article twice.</li>
<li><strong>Research first.</strong> The language model searches the web and collects facts before it writes. If fewer than two sources can be found and opened, the article is rejected.</li>
<li><strong>Sources you can check.</strong> The sources are listed at the end of every article under "Sources", and the text should name where each fact comes from.</li>
<li><strong>Checks before publishing.</strong> An article is rejected if it claims personal testing or experience ("I tested"), has too few sources, or contains visible formatting errors. Sentences with vague sources such as "experts say" are sent back to be rewritten with a named source.</li>
<li><strong>Links.</strong> Links inside the text go only to sources that were checked, or to other articles on this site about a related subject.</li>
<li><strong>Images and video.</strong> Images are illustrations made with AI, under rules that forbid real people, text, numbers and charts, so they are not mistaken for evidence. Some articles include a YouTube video by an independent creator when it explains the subject well.</li>
</ol>
<h2>Independence and money</h2>
<p>Tech Media Arch earns money from ads placed automatically by Google and from affiliate links in some articles. Every article carries an affiliate disclosure, and articles built around affiliate or referral links (Resources) have it at the top. Nobody can pay to be covered or to get a favorable review. Read more in our <a href="/p/disclosure">Disclosure</a>.</p>
<h2>Corrections</h2>
<p>If you find an error, write to <a href="mailto:info@techmediaarch.com">info@techmediaarch.com</a> with the link to the article and what is wrong (see <a href="/p/contact-us">Contact Us</a>). Errors are checked and fixed, and a corrected article shows an "Updated" date next to the publication date.</p>
<h2>What Tech Media Arch is not</h2>
<p>Tech Media Arch is a blog, not a news agency. It does not interview sources and is not a member of a press complaints body. Articles are general information, not financial, legal or security advice for your situation.</p>
