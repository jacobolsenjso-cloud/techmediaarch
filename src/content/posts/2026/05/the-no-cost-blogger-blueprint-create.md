<h2>Setting Up the Course Foundation on Blogger</h2>

<p>According to Google Help, anyone with a Google Account can <a href="https://support.google.com/blogger/answer/1623800?hl=en" rel="noopener" target="_blank">launch a website</a> without paying monthly hosting fees. A new site is created by clicking the blog dropdown, selecting the option for a new blog, and entering a course title.</p>

<p>The creator must then assign a unique blog web address ending in the Blogspot domain. This free web address lets teachers launch public or private classes right away without buying a custom domain.</p>

<p>Google runs the entire publishing system on its own cloud servers. Because of this, users do not need to purchase separate server contracts, bandwidth plans, or database maintenance tools.</p>

<p>Creators also get full access to dashboard design tools and template files without buying expensive software upgrades. The platform allows full customization of page layouts, gadgets, and design themes from the first day.</p>

<h2>Structuring Pages, Lessons, and Curriculum Navigation</h2>

<p>The course is organized into two parts: standalone pages that do not change and standard blog posts arranged by date.</p>

<p>Static pages work best for permanent reference items like syllabi, course rules, and teacher biographies. These pages live outside the main stream of daily updates so students can find basic details quickly.</p>

<p>Individual video lessons are published as standard blog posts. Educational guides from Marketingmalone explain that adding labels to lesson posts lets teachers group content into specific modules or units.</p>

<p>Instructors can display a clear course list by adding navigation buttons and menus through the layout dashboard. Here are four common components used to build <a href="https://marketingmalone.com/blog/tutorial-create-a-basic-navigation-menu-in-blogger/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">clear course navigation</a>:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong><a href="https://makingamark.blogspot.com/2010/02/how-to-make-blogger-pages-work-for-you.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Pages Gadget</a>:</strong> Displays top navigation tabs or sidebar links that lead directly to core syllabus pages.</li>
<li><strong>LinkList Widget:</strong> Creates an ordered list of hyperlinks guiding students toward specific lesson tracks.</li>
<li><strong>Custom Labels:</strong> Tags individual posts so students can click a topic and filter related lessons.</li>
<li><strong>Post Display Limits:</strong> Adjusts the maximum number of lessons shown on the main homepage.</li>
</ul>

<p>Teachers adjust the home feed under the settings menu by modifying the maximum posts shown on the main page. This setting keeps the front screen neat and easy for students to read.</p>

<h2>Controlling Lesson Order and Chronology</h2>

<p>Standard blogs display new entries at the top of the page in reverse-chronological order. This default display can confuse students who need to complete lessons in a specific order.</p>

<p>A technical guide from MAKING A MARK notes that changing post dates lets teachers organize lessons in order, rather than showing the newest ones first.</p>

<p>Instructors can backdate earlier lessons or set future release dates to keep every post in the proper spot. This simple schedule change keeps Module One sitting permanently ahead of Module Two.</p>

<p>Authors create these lessons using two different editing views in the post dashboard. The Compose view provides a normal rich-text workspace with standard styling buttons for writing text.</p>

<p>The HTML view allows creators to paste custom embed codes and script tags directly into the lesson. Switching between these two views takes only a single click in the top corner of the editor.</p>

<h2>Video Hosting Options: YouTube vs. Direct Storage</h2>

<p>Blogger includes a native video button inside the post editor. This button lets users upload files from a computer or select existing clips from a YouTube channel.</p>

<p>Video files uploaded directly to Blogger count against a creator's shared cloud storage limit, according to MAKING A MARK. Every standard Google Account includes 15 GB of free space shared across Gmail, Drive, and Photos.</p>

<p>Documentation from Google Help shows that making a video unlisted hides it from general search results while still letting it play smoothly on other websites. Unlisted clips do not count against the 15 GB account storage quota.</p>

<p>Here are the main differences between how unlisted and private video options work:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Unlisted Videos:</strong> These clips do not appear in channel searches, still allow comments, and let unlimited students watch embedded videos without logging in.</li>
<li><strong>Private Videos:</strong> These clips restrict access to at most 50 specific email addresses and turn off viewer comments completely.</li>
<li><strong>Direct Blogger Uploads:</strong> These files play through the built-in player, but they use up your shared cloud storage.</li>
</ul>

<p>Because unlisted YouTube videos have no viewer limits, teachers can share video lectures with large classes without running out of storage space.</p>

<p>Instructors can also store video lectures in Google Drive and insert them into blog entries. Clicking the three-dot menu on a video file opens an embed option that provides an iframe snippet.</p>

<p>Analysis from Swarmify explains that Google Drive embeds use a preview link and limit streaming video quality to 1080p. The video frame displays cleanly inside any standard post.</p>

<p>Swarmify also points out that Google Drive has hidden view limits that can temporarily stop video playback during sudden traffic spikes. These temporary playback locks generally clear within 24 hours.</p>

<p>Creators who prefer other media players can switch the post editor to HTML mode and paste external embed code. This flexibility allows teachers to use different video networks without changing their blog layout.</p>

<p>Instructors can review all video media uploaded directly to their blog under the manage blog section. From there, creators can safely review, download, or delete past video uploads.</p>

<h2>Embedding Quizzes and Interactive Worksheets</h2>

<p>Instructors can build free interactive quizzes using Google Forms and insert them into any lesson post.</p>

<p>Opening the send menu inside Google Forms generates an iframe code under the embed tab. Pasting this code into the Blogger HTML view displays the form right below the video player.</p>

<p>Reporting from WebsitePublisher.ai highlights that answers from embedded test forms automatically go into a spreadsheet for immediate review. Students receive their test results immediately, which reinforces their understanding of the material.</p>

<p>Instructors can also share supplementary study materials by storing PDFs and presentation slides in Google Drive. Pasting sharing links into posts gives students fast access to course handouts and worksheets.</p>

<p>Course creators can lock down their content so only enrolled students can view the lessons.</p>

<p>As detailed by Google Help, teachers can restrict who can see the blog by choosing custom reader access in the settings. This feature blocks general web traffic from viewing lesson pages.</p>

<p>Course owners invite students by adding their email addresses under the permissions tab. The platform sends an automated invitation link that remains valid for 30 days.</p>

<p>Students must accept the invitation within that time window using their personal Google Account. Once approved, only logged-in students can view the course content.</p>

<p>Blogger limits private membership to a total of 100 members. That maximum figure includes blog administrators, contributing authors, and invited student readers combined.</p>

<p>This user cap makes private Blogger setups best suited for small cohorts, tutoring groups, or private internal training programs. Large open courses must remain public to reach bigger audiences.</p>

<h2>The Technical Breakdown: Custom Domains and Security Setup</h2>

<p>Instructors who want a professional web presence can connect a custom domain to their course. Setting up a domain requires updating specific Domain Name System (DNS) records through the company where you bought your domain.</p>

<p>Creators must add two distinct CNAME records in their registrar management console. One record points the www address to Google servers, while the second record proves you own the site by using a unique code of letters and numbers.</p>

<p>Connecting an apex domain (a domain without 'www') requires adding four specific A-records pointing to Google IP addresses, according to technical guides from MAKING A MARK. These four records point the root web address directly to the following servers:</p>

<ul>
<li><code>216.239.32.21</code></li>
<li><code>216.239.34.21</code></li>
<li><code>216.239.36.21</code></li>
<li><code>216.239.38.21</code></li>
</ul>

<p>Blogger provides free, automatic SSL certificates to protect and encrypt web traffic on custom domains. Domain security records must approve the certificate provider to keep continuous HTTPS protection turned on for students.</p>

<p>Specifically, any Certification Authority Authorization (CAA) security record on the domain must include an entry for letsencrypt.org. Once active, the entire site loads safely through secure connections.</p>

<h3>Can an online video course be hosted on Blogger for free?</h3>
<p>Yes. Blogger provides completely free hosting on Google's cloud servers, with no monthly subscription fees or software licenses. Creators can host course videos on YouTube at no cost and link study files through Google Drive.</p>

<h3>How are video lessons embedded into Blogger posts?</h3>
<p>Instructors click the built-in video icon in the post toolbar to upload files directly or pick videos from their YouTube channel. They can also switch the post editor into HTML view and paste iframe embed codes from third-party players or Google Drive.</p>

<h3>How can an instructor organize lessons into a sequential curriculum?</h3>
<p>Teachers use labels to group posts into organized course tracks and modules. Blogger normally puts the newest posts first. Because of this, teachers change the date and time on each post so lessons appear in order from first to last.</p>

<h3>What is the maximum number of students allowed in a private course?</h3>
<p>A private Blogger site supports a maximum of 100 total members. This combined limit includes all site administrators, authorized authors, and invited custom readers.</p>

<h3>How do instructors add quizzes and assignments to video lessons?</h3>
<p>Instructors create quizzes in Google Forms, copy the embed iframe code, and paste it into the Blogger HTML editor. Student quiz answers and homework scores automatically save to a connected Google Sheet in real time.</p>

<h3>Do students need a Google Account to watch unlisted course videos?</h3>
<p>No. Unlisted YouTube videos embedded on a public Blogger site do not require viewers to log in to an account.
