faq:
  - q: What causes toxic backlinks to damage a website's reputation?
    a: Toxic backlinks originate from untrustworthy sources like hacked websites, shady directories, or link farms, which Google views with suspicion. Having too many of these low-quality connections pointing to a website can cause Google to lower search rankings or apply a penalty. Protecting the site requires identifying and eliminating these harmful elements.
  - q: How can webmasters use Google Search Console to examine their backlink profile?
    a: Google Search Console functions as a free health report card for websites. By navigating to the designated links section, administrators can review which external pages point to their domain and identify unnatural or unfamiliar sources.
  - q: What third-party services can help uncover high-risk backlinks?
    a: Platform services such as Ahrefs, SEMrush, and Moz assist administrators in digging deeper into their link profiles. These tools allow users to sort references by authority, relevance, or specific spam scores to pinpoint potential threats.
  - q: When does Google recommend utilizing the Disavow Tool?
    a: Google advises using the Disavow Tool specifically when a website accumulates a substantial number of artificial or low-quality links that negatively impact rankings. This step is necessary only after attempts to contact the respective site owners for manual removal have failed.
  - q: What format is required for the text file uploaded to the Disavow Tool?
    a: The plain text file listing unwanted links or entire domains must be formatted carefully, with each individual entry placed on a new line before submission to the Google Disavow Links Tool.
