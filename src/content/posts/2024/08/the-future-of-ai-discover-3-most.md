<li>Generative AI can create new content, such as art, music, and even entire stories, from scratch.</li>
<li>AI-powered gadgets are making everyday tasks easier and more efficient, from smart home devices to wearable tech.</li>

<li>Understanding and adapting to these AI innovations can help us better prepare for the future.</li></ul><h2>1. Multimodality</h2><p>




<img alt="Futuristic city with AI robots and holograms" src="/images/b24bc556abd03cba.jpg" style="max-height: 200px; max-width: 100%;" />

</p><p>Multimodality might sound like jargon, but it’s worth understanding what it means: it’s the ability of an AI system to process lots of different types of data—not just text, but also images, video, audio, and more.</p><p>This year was the first time that the public gained access to powerful multimodal AI models. <a data-preview="" href="https://www.google.com/search?ved=1t:260882&amp;q=OpenAI+GPT-4+capabilities&amp;bbid=427886895467585040&amp;bpid=1074236557828118414" target="_blank">OpenAI’s GPT-4</a> was the first of these, allowing users to upload images as well as text inputs. GPT-4 can “see” the contents of an image, which opens up all kinds of possibilities, for example, asking it what to make for dinner based on a photograph of the contents of your fridge. In September, OpenAI rolled out the ability for users to interact with ChatGPT by voice as well as text.</p><p><a data-preview="" href="https://www.google.com/search?ved=1t:260882&amp;q=Google+DeepMind+Gemini+capabilities&amp;bbid=427886895467585040&amp;bpid=1074236557828118414" target="_blank">Google DeepMind’s latest model, Gemini</a>, announced in December, can also work with images and audio. A launch video shared by Google showed the model identifying a duck based on a line drawing on a Post-it note. In the same video, after being shown an image of pink and blue yarn and asked what it could be used to make, Gemini generated an image of a pink-and-blue octopus plushie. (The marketing video appeared to show Gemini observing moving images and responding to audio commands in real time, but in a post on its website, Google said the video had been edited for brevity—and that the model was being prompted using still images, not video, and text prompts, not audio, although the model does have audio capabilities.)</p><p>“I think the next landmark that people will think back to, and remember, is [AI systems] going much more fully multimodal,” Google DeepMind co-founder <a data-preview="" href="https://www.google.com/search?ved=1t:260882&amp;q=Shane+Legg&amp;bbid=427886895467585040&amp;bpid=1074236557828118414" target="_blank">Shane Legg</a> said on a podcast in October. “It’s early days in this transition, and when you start really digesting a lot of video and other things like that, these systems will start having a much more grounded understanding of the world.” In an interview with TIME in November, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman said multimodality in the company’s new models would be one of the key things to watch out for next year.</p><h2>2. Generative AI</h2><p>Generative AI has become a game-changer in recent years. <strong>According to a <a data-preview="" href="https://www.google.com/search?ved=1t:260882&amp;q=PwC+report+AI+economic+impact&amp;bbid=427886895467585040&amp;bpid=1074236557828118414" target="_blank">PwC report</a>, <a href="https://etedge-insights.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/how-generative-ai-is-redefining-the-future-of-innovation/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">AI could contribute up to $15.7 trillion to the global economy by 2030</a></strong>, with generative AI playing a central role in this transformation. This technology is not just about creating text or images; it's about integrating seamlessly into our daily lives and workflows.</p><p>Generative AI tools are now embedded in many enterprise environments. For example, <a data-preview="" href="https://www.google.com/search?ved=1t:260882&amp;q=Microsoft+Office+Copilot+features&amp;bbid=427886895467585040&amp;bpid=1074236557828118414" target="_blank">Microsoft Office's "Copilot" features</a> and Adobe Photoshop's "Generative Fill" are enhancing productivity without replacing existing tools. These integrations show that the real impact of generative AI lies in its ability to complement and enhance current systems.</p><p>The evolution of generative AI mirrors the development of computers. Initially, only a few had access to massive, centrally operated mainframes. Over time, smaller and more efficient machines became available to more people. Today, generative AI is in its "hobbyist" phase, with open-source models like <a data-preview="" href="https://www.google.com/search?ved=1t:260882&amp;q=Meta+LLaMa+open+source+model&amp;bbid=427886895467585040&amp;bpid=1074236557828118414" target="_blank">Meta’s LlaMa</a> and <a data-preview="" href="https://www.google.com/search?ved=1t:260882&amp;q=Stable+Diffusion+AI+model&amp;bbid=427886895467585040&amp;bpid=1074236557828118414" target="_blank">Stable Diffusion</a> making advanced AI accessible to a broader audience.</p><blockquote>
The next wave of generative AI advancements will focus on making the technology more trustworthy, sustainable, and accessible. This includes improvements in governance, middleware, training techniques, and data pipelines.
</blockquote><p>In summary, generative AI is not just a fleeting trend. Its integration into everyday tools and its potential to drive economic growth make it a pivotal technology for the future.</p><h2>3. AI-Powered Gadgets</h2><p>
